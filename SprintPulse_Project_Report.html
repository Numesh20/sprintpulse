1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

1.1 The Industry Problem

In fast-paced software engineering environments (e.g., IFS, Sysco LABS, Virtusa, WSO2, 99x), engineering teams work in 2-week agile sprints. However, delivery unpredictability and silent scope creep frequently derail milestone commitments.

Traditional tools like Jira and Azure DevOps store vast amounts of raw ticketing data, but require Project Managers to spend 3 to 5 hours per week manually extracting metrics into slide decks for executive reporting. Furthermore, these tools lack automated heuristics to detect delivery risks (e.g., Pull Requests stagnating in code review or developer overallocation) before they cause sprint failure.

1.2 The SprintPulse Solution

SprintPulse is a unified Agile Project Management and Sprint Velocity Analytics platform that:

  • Computes a Single Sprint Health Index (0–100): Evaluates burndown adherence, velocity predictability, mid-sprint scope volatility, and code review lead times in real time.
  • Automated Risk & Bottleneck Radar: Uses rule-based heuristics to flag developer overallocation, review bottlenecks (>48h), and unestimated backlog tasks.
  • Integrated Ceremony Toolkit: Provides digital tooling for Fibonacci Planning Poker, Sprint Retrospectives with upvoting, and RAID log tracking.
  • One-Click Stakeholder Reporting: Generates formatted executive briefing documents in Markdown and PDF formats.

2. Agile Metrics, Mathematical Formulations & KPI Models

SprintPulse implements established Agile and Scrum measurement standards. These formulas are implemented in js/metrics.js and js/riskEngine.js.

2.1 Velocity & Predictability Ratio

Committed Velocity ($V_{\text{committed}}$): Total Story Points agreed upon during Sprint Planning. Completed Velocity ($V_{\text{completed}}$): Story Points meeting the team's Definition of Done (DoD).

Sprint Predictability Ratio (%) = min(100, (Completed SP / Committed SP) * 100)

Industry Benchmark: A predictability ratio between 85% and 100% indicates mature sprint estimation and stable capacity planning.

2.2 Scope Creep & Volatility Index (%)

Measures the volume of unplanned user stories injected into the sprint backlog after sprint kickoff:

Scope Volatility Index (%) = (Story Points Added Mid-Sprint / Initial Committed SP) * 100

Thresholds: < 5%: Low (Healthy) • 5%–14%: Moderate (Warning) • ≥ 15%: High (Critical Escalation Required).

2.3 Daily Burndown Trajectory & Variance

The ideal linear burndown trajectory is computed as:

Ideal Remaining (Day d) = Committed SP - ((Committed SP / Total Days) * d)

If the actual remaining story points exceed the ideal baseline by > 18% past Day 5 of the sprint, the system flags a burndown trajectory slippage risk.

2.4 Composite Sprint Health Score (0–100)

SprintPulse computes an overall health index starting at 100 points and applying weighted penalties:

  • Burndown Delay Penalty: Up to -25 points based on daily schedule variance.
  • Scope Creep Penalty: Up to -20 points based on mid-sprint backlog additions.
  • Overallocation Penalty: -8 points per team member assigned points exceeding 100% capacity.
  • Review Bottleneck Penalty: -10 points per Pull Request remaining in review for >48 hours.

4.2 Interactive Ceremony Modules in Action

1. Interactive Sprint Retrospective Board

Facilitates the continuous improvement ceremony. Team members post cards into What Went Well (Green), What Needs Improvement (Orange), and Action Items for Next Sprint (Cyan). Cards can be upvoted dynamically, sorted by popularity, and saved to localStorage.

2. Fibonacci Planning Poker Story Point Estimator

Allows Scrum teams to estimate user stories collaboratively using the Fibonacci scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, ?, Pass). When cards are revealed, the engine calculates the average estimate, highlights outlier votes, and indicates team consensus.

3. RAID Log Manager (5x5 Qualitative Matrix)

Enables PMs to log and categorize Risks, Assumptions, Issues, and Dependencies. Each item is scored on Likelihood (1–5) and Impact (1–5), generating a severity level (Critical, High, Medium, Low) with mitigation action tracking.

Use these talking points when interviewing with tech companies in Sri Lanka (IFS, Sysco LABS, Virtusa, WSO2, 99x, Axiata Digital Labs, Pearson) or global remote teams:

Q1: "Can you walk us through a project management project you worked on?"
Answer: "I designed and implemented SprintPulse, an Agile Velocity & Team Health Analytics platform. I acted as the Associate IT Project Manager, formulating the full SDLC governance suite—including a Project Charter with a RACI matrix, a Product Requirements Document with MoSCoW prioritization, a 5x5 RAID Risk Register, and a 4-Level WBS. I also developed the web application to calculate real-time Burndown trajectories, Velocity trends, and automated heuristic risk detection for overallocated developers and PR review bottlenecks."
Q2: "How do you detect and handle scope creep during a sprint?"
Answer: "In SprintPulse, I built a Scope Volatility Index that tracks points added mid-sprint against the initial commitment. When additions exceed 10%, the system flags a risk alert. As a PM, my approach is to enforce a strict MoSCoW change request process: if a new critical story must enter the sprint, we collaborate with the Product Owner to swap out an equivalent story point value of lower-priority work to protect team capacity."
Q3: "How do you ensure follow-through on Sprint Retrospective action items?"
Answer: "During retrospectives, we capture feedback under 'Went Well', 'To Improve', and 'Action Items' with team upvoting. In SprintPulse, each action item requires an explicit assignee (e.g., Tech Lead or DevOps). At the start of the next Sprint Planning ceremony, these action items are converted into backlog tasks or Definition of Done checklist updates to ensure continuous improvement."
